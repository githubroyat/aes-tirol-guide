Four things to know first

Any EB pass gets you into an Austrian university.Austrian law treats the European Baccalaureate as a general university entrance qualification. Your subjects and grades don't change that.

Understand

How the EB works

The European Baccalaureate is the diploma at the end of S7. This page shows what it opens, how it is examined, and which rules lock in your choices early.

In short

  • Any EB pass is enough to apply.Austrian law treats the EB as a general university entrance qualification.
  • Some degrees add one exam.Latin for medicine, law and many humanities. Descriptive geometry for architecture and engineering.
  • Exams make up half the mark.Five written and three oral exams count 50%. Your S7 preliminary mark counts the other 50%.

One key, a few extra locks

Think of the EB as a key to the main door of every Austrian university. Some degrees have an extra lock, a . The right school subjects open it for you. Without them, you pass the exam at university.

Most degrees

Economics, computer science, psychology, physics, chemistry and many more.

No subject exam
Medicine, law, humanities

10 weekly hours of Latin at school open this lock. Otherwise you take a Latin exam during your studies.

Latin
Classical philology, teaching Latin

Here Latin has to be done before you can enrol. Innsbruck's archaeology programmes take it during studies instead.

Latin first
Architecture and engineering

The EB doesn't teach , so AES Tirol pupils who need it take this exam at university.

Descriptive geometry

These locks are about school subjects. Separately, some degrees have entrance tests for everyone: medicine (MedAT, in German only), psychology, teacher training, architecture and sport. And degrees taught in German need German proof (B2 at Uni Innsbruck, C1 for medicine). The Degrees in Austria page shows all three for each degree.

8 exams at the end of S7

Five written exams and three oral exams. L1 is examined both in writing and orally. Oral 2 is L2, History or Geography; History, Geography and your oral-3 subject can't be ones you also sit in writing.

Rules that lock you in

These come from the official European Schools rules. They're why early choices matter.

Languages of teaching

European Schools teach several subjects in your first foreign language, your . This is the official language policy, not a school choice. Every exam is taken in the language the subject was taught in.

In short

  • Your L1 decides your L2.German L1 means L2 English. English L1 means L2 German.
  • History, Geography and Economics use your L2.They are taught and examined in it. History and Geography are never taught in L1.
  • L2 aims for C1.That is the target by the end of S7, and the written L2 exam is marked at C1 level.

  • At AES Tirol the secondary sections are German and English, so for most pupils L2 is the other of the two.
  • L2 aims for C1 by the end of S7, according to the official L2 syllabus. The written L2 exam is marked at C1 level. L2 Advanced aims for C1+.
  • L2 can change only on entry to S6, and even then History, Geography and Economics stay in the old L2.
  • Small groups: if a 4-lesson science or Philosophy can't run in your L1, you may follow it in another language you know. In the exam you may then ask for a bilingual dictionary.
  • Pupils without their own section (for example Italian speakers) keep their home language as L1 and learn most other subjects in the section language.

        Medicine in detail

        The MedAT decides

        Public medical universities don't use school grades. Places go by the score. This is how the 2026 test for human medicine (MedAT-H) is weighted:

        Dentistry (MedAT-Z) swaps text comprehension and part of the cognitive section for manual skills (20%). Your EB sciences are the preparation for the 40% science part. The test is in German only.

        The medicine quota

        For human medicine, the law keeps places by type of school certificate. Each square is 5% of places.

        At least 75% for holders of Austrian school certificates Up to 20% for other EU citizens At most 5% for everyone else

        A MedUni Wien checklist puts the European Baccalaureate in the Austrian group (the IB Diploma is not). The checklist says it isn't legally binding, and this quota law runs out on 31 December 2027, just before the first AES Tirol group applies.

        Proof, tests and English-taught options

        Medicine is German-only

        • The MedAT and its preparation materials are "solely available in German" (official MedAT site).
        • Admission to medicine and dentistry needs German at C1, proven when you take up your place, not when you register for the test.
        • The only English-taught route found: Karl Landsteiner University, Krems, Bachelor Medical Science (own written test and interview, €11,000 per semester). Its master's in human medicine asks for German B2. Its bachelor also asks for 10 weekly hours of school Latin, or a Latin/medical-terminology test before the end of the degree.

        What counts as German proof

        • The ministry's recognition note on the EB: "If the subject German appears on the EB certificate, German is thereby proven." It names no level and makes no L1/L2 distinction.
        • The universities' own lists name a certificate from a school with German as the language of teaching and exams, a language certificate (ÖSD, Goethe, telc, TestDaF), or their German supplementary exam. None mentions the EB. A few accept German as a school subject (see the table above).
        • German section: German is the language of teaching and exams. English section: German is L2, examined at C1, with History, Geography and Economics taught in German.
        • Language certificates expire after two or three years, depending on the university, so take one in S7 rather than earlier.
        • Without proof you sit a German supplementary exam (Vorstudienlehrgang) before admission. You need A2 to apply.

        English-taught bachelor's

        • WU Vienna, Business and Economics: no German needed. A pass in English on a school-leaving certificate from the EU, EEA or Switzerland counts as English proof.
        • MCI Innsbruck, Business & Management: full-time and entirely in English, €363.36 per semester for EU citizens. Applications in English.
        • Karl Landsteiner University: Medical Science (see "Medicine is German-only").
        • Uni Innsbruck: all bachelor's degrees are taught in German. English-taught programmes are mostly master's.
        • These programmes still have their own application steps.

        Entrance tests are mostly in German

        • MedAT: German only.
        • Vetmeduni aptitude test: held in German.
        • Psychology at Innsbruck: texts in German and English.
        • Teacher training: the degree is taught in German, and the test checks language skills among others.

        Tips for the English section

        • German stays your L2 to the end. That gives you German on the EB certificate, and History, Geography and Economics in German.
        • L2 Advanced (German) aims for C1+ and adds a German written exam.
        • History 4, Geography 4 or Economics add German lessons, and a written exam in German if you choose it.
        • For medicine, or for Vienna and Graz, plan a C1 certificate (ÖSD or Goethe) in S7 as a backup.

        Do EB grades matter in Austria?

        • Hardly. No Austrian procedure found ranks applicants by EB grade.
        • Medicine, psychology and WU rank by test score. Universities of applied sciences mainly use interviews.
        • Grades matter more abroad: see EB grades by country.

        Act

        Deadlines

        From handing in the S6–S7 choice form to applying for university: what happens when.

        In short

        • The S6–S7 form is due Friday 27 November 2026.It goes to your homeroom teacher and binds you for both years.
        • Changes are only possible between S6 and S7.You can drop Maths 5 to 3, or a 4-lesson subject to 2 lessons; moving up needs a test. Nothing new starts in S7.
        • Exam subjects are final.You register them by 15 October of S7 and can't change them afterwards.

        From the choice form to university

        1. Now
          Start from your L1, then choose S6–S7 subjects

          After L1 come Maths, your four-lesson electives (at least 2, at most 4) and at most one complementary subject. Check the prerequisites: L3, Latin, Economics, Language 4, Art and Music need the same subject in S4–S5.

        2. 27 Nov
          Hand in the S6–S7 choice form

          Due Friday 27 November 2026, to your homeroom teacher. Options run only if at least 5 pupils choose them.

        3. S6
          The EB years begin

          Your choices are binding. At the end of S6 you can drop Maths 5 to Maths 3, or a 4-lesson subject to its 2-lesson course; moving up needs an attainment test.

        4. S7
          By 15 October: register your exam subjects

          Five written and three oral exams. The choice is final.

        5. January
          Long Pre-Bac exams

          Usually two weeks after the winter holidays. With your S7 class marks they make up the preliminary mark (40% class marks, 60% Pre-Bac), which counts 50% of the final mark.

        6. June
          Written exams, then orals

          Written exams in the first two weeks of June, at most two a day. Orals follow on the school's own schedule: 20 minutes each, after 20 minutes of preparation. Written exams count 35%, orals 15%. With a justified reason, a missed written exam can be taken as a substitute exam later in the session; the Handbook also mentions September.

        7. After
          Apply

          Have your German proof ready. Medicine: register for the MedAT in March, sit it in July.

        Due Friday 27 November 2026

        The 2027/28 S6–S7 choice form goes to your homeroom teacher. The choice is binding for S6 and S7. Form: agibk.at downloads, "Fächerwahl".

        The first EB group

        Today's S6 (school year 2026/27) is the first AES Tirol group to sit the EB, in 2028. Their exam choices are due by 15 October 2027.

        Things that may change

        Recommended 14 Oct 2026

        Latin rules may relax

        The rectors' conference (uniko) recommended dropping Latin for law, medicine, Protestant theology and all teaching subjects except Latin, among others. Latin would stay for philosophy, history, art history and Catholic theology. Two ministries must agree. Nothing has changed yet, so plan with today's rules.

        Ends 31 Dec 2027 (UG)

        The medicine quota

        The 75% quota for Austrian certificates is set to expire before the first AES Tirol group applies in 2028. It may be renewed or changed.

        Decided each spring

        Entrance procedures

        Uni Innsbruck decides every year which entrance procedures run. Biology, computer science, pharmacy and economics were suspended for 2026/27. The 2027/28 details aren't published yet.
